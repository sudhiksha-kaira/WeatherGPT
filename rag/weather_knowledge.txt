WEATHERGPT WEATHER AND CLIMATE KNOWLEDGE BASE

1. WEATHER

Weather describes the current condition of the atmosphere at a particular place and time.

Important weather parameters include temperature, humidity, atmospheric pressure, wind speed, wind direction, cloud cover, and precipitation.

Temperature describes how hot or cold the atmosphere is.

Humidity describes the amount of water vapor present in the air.

Atmospheric pressure is the force exerted by the weight of air in the atmosphere.

Wind is the movement of air from an area of higher pressure toward an area of lower pressure.

Precipitation includes rain, snow, sleet, and hail that fall from the atmosphere.


2. CLIMATE

Climate describes the long-term pattern of weather in a particular region.

Weather can change within minutes or hours, while climate is studied over much longer periods.

Climate is influenced by latitude, altitude, distance from oceans, landforms, ocean currents, and atmospheric circulation.


3. MONSOON

The Indian monsoon is a seasonal wind system that brings major changes in rainfall across India.

The southwest monsoon generally brings most of India's annual rainfall.

Monsoon rainfall is important for agriculture, water resources, reservoirs, and ecosystems.

The timing and amount of monsoon rainfall can vary between regions and years.


4. EL NINO

El Niño is a climate pattern associated with unusually warm sea surface temperatures in the central and eastern tropical Pacific Ocean.

El Niño can influence atmospheric circulation and weather patterns around the world.

Its effects on Indian monsoon rainfall can vary depending on the event and other climate factors.


5. LA NINA

La Niña is a climate pattern associated with unusually cool sea surface temperatures in the central and eastern tropical Pacific Ocean.

La Niña can influence global atmospheric circulation and weather patterns.

Its influence on Indian monsoon rainfall can vary from one event to another.


6. HEATWAVE

A heatwave is a period of unusually high temperatures that persists for several days.

Heatwaves can increase the risk of dehydration, heat exhaustion, and heat-related illness.

During extreme heat, people should stay hydrated, reduce unnecessary outdoor exposure, and follow official local health advisories.


7. THUNDERSTORM

A thunderstorm is a storm associated with lightning and thunder.

Thunderstorms can produce heavy rain, strong winds, lightning, and sometimes hail.

During thunderstorms, people should avoid exposed outdoor areas and seek shelter in a safe building.


8. CYCLONE

A tropical cyclone is a large rotating weather system that forms over warm tropical or subtropical ocean waters.

Cyclones can produce strong winds, heavy rainfall, flooding, and storm surges.

People in affected areas should follow official warnings and evacuation instructions issued by local authorities.


9. FLOOD

Flooding occurs when water covers land that is normally dry.

Heavy rainfall, overflowing rivers, storm surges, and drainage problems can contribute to flooding.

During floods, people should avoid walking or driving through moving floodwater and follow local emergency instructions.


10. DROUGHT

A drought is a prolonged period of unusually low rainfall that results in water shortages.

Droughts can affect agriculture, drinking water supplies, ecosystems, and local economies.

Water conservation and effective water-resource management can help reduce drought impacts.


11. WEATHER FORECAST

A weather forecast is an estimate of future atmospheric conditions.

Forecasts may include temperature, precipitation probability, wind speed, humidity, pressure, and weather conditions.

Forecast accuracy generally decreases as the forecast period becomes longer.


12. RAIN PROBABILITY

Rain probability represents the likelihood of measurable precipitation occurring at a specified location during a specified period.

A higher probability indicates a greater chance of precipitation, but it does not necessarily indicate how much rain will fall.


13. WEATHER SAFETY

During severe weather, people should follow official warnings from trusted meteorological and emergency-management authorities.

Weather applications can provide useful information, but emergency decisions should be based on current official alerts and instructions.
